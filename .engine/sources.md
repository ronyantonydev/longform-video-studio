# Free sources (always check the licence on each file)

## Video and photos (video first)
Archive films: National Archives catalog, Internet Archive (Prelinger and US government films; check
rights with step `ia`), USDA, NRCS, National Park Service, US Fish and Wildlife Service.
Wikimedia Commons, NASA, NOAA, USGS, USDA, DVIDS, Voice of America,
ESA, Internet Archive (check each item), Smithsonian Open Access, Library of Congress,
YouTube clips marked Creative Commons (verify; uploaders sometimes mislabel).
Images: Google Gemini first (Gemini API with the Google AI Studio key). Check the current
free-tier limits and per-image price before each run; an unknown price stops the run.
Backup: Google Flow's image model (signed in through Chrome).

## Sound: music and effects (free options)
- YouTube Audio Library: free, needs a Google account in YouTube Studio; some tracks need credit.
- Freesound: sound effects, CC0 or CC-BY (credit required for BY); has an API key.
- Mixkit: free music and effects under its own free licence.
- Free Music Archive and Incompetech: Creative Commons, usually CC-BY, so the credit line is required.
- Epidemic Sound: paid subscription; optional.
Notes: free tracks can still trigger YouTube Content ID claims, so keep the track name, source
and licence in the ledger. Put required credits in youtube-description.txt.

## Voice (free options)
Google AI Studio voice (free tier, needs a Google account API key), Kokoro (local), Piper (local),
macOS built-in voices (robotic). Paid: ElevenLabs, OpenAI.
